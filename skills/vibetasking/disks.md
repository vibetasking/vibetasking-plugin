# Named disks

Named disks are the workspace's own folders of files, which the app calls Knowledge: documents, notes and pages that people and agents read with the same file tools they use for everything else. Every workspace has them, and one of them may be its brain (**The brain** below).

## Making one

`create_disk` with a ``name``, one folder name unique in the workspace, and a ``description`` of what it holds, which every run that mounts it reads. With ``private`` true, the default, it is yours: you and whoever oversees you (the workspace's owners, and its admins over a member) read and write it until you share it. With ``private`` false it is the whole workspace's: its admins and owners write it and decide who reads it, and every seat reads it from the start through its share with the workspace (**Sharing it** below), which they may take away to keep it to themselves and the people they share it with. With ``brain`` true it is the company's brain, one per workspace and always the whole workspace's, which an owner makes while the workspace has its Brain on, and which its owners alone keep (**The brain** below). Nothing exists until someone makes one.

## Who reads and writes a path

Path rules keep a file or a folder (a path ending in ``/``) to some of the disk's readers, with `create_disk_rule`. A ``READABLE`` or ``WRITABLE`` rule names one subject: a ``role`` (``MEMBER``, ``ADMIN`` or ``OWNER``) and the seats above it, a ``member_id``, the members of a ``scim_group_id`` when the workspace provisions groups, or an ``agent_id``, whose conversations nobody starts it names. A path's rules of a type add up, so each subject takes a rule of its own, and the workspace's owners read and write every path.

- ``READABLE``: only who its rules name, and their conversations, read what the path holds. Everyone who reads the disk still sees its files by name and their line in ``index.md``, and a conversation sees them as empty files it cannot open.
- ``WRITABLE``: only who its rules name write the path, everyone else reading it.
- ``LOCKED``: no conversation changes the path, while people still do. With an ``agent_id``, that one agent's conversations still change it. A path holds one lock, so to spare another agent change the one it has.

A rule naming a seat is set and removed only by someone at or above it, and no rule keeps a path from the person setting it. `update_disk_rule` moves a seat rule to another seat, from someone at or above both, or changes the agent a lock spares, and `delete_disk_rule` removes one. A rule the brain's curator set reads ``curator`` true until a person changes it.

## Sharing it

- **With people**: `create_grant` on the disk (``VIEWER`` reads it, ``EDITOR`` writes it too, ``MANAGER`` also shares it, sets its path rules and deletes it). A ``VIEWER`` or ``EDITOR`` grant may name one folder in ``path``, which that person alone then reads or writes. A disk every seat reads takes no ``VIEWER`` grant.
- **With the whole workspace**: `create_mount` with no target, the disk's ``disk_id``, ``path`` its own folder ``disks/<name>/`` and ``mode`` ``WRITE``, which whoever manages the disk makes. A disk of the whole workspace starts with one. Every seat reads the disk while it stands, people who join later included, and each writes it as far as their own rights reach. `delete_mount` on it keeps the disk to whoever manages it and the people it is shared with, and takes off what others attached while it lasted.
- **With an agent's runs**: an agent's run, whoever starts it, reads a named disk only when `create_mount` attaches it to that agent, ``agent_id`` set and ``path`` the disk's folder. ``mode`` ``READ`` keeps every run of the agent to reading it. ``mode`` ``WRITE`` lets a run a person starts write it as far as that person may, while a conversation nobody starts (a channel, a trigger nobody owns) still only reads it. `update_mount` changes the mode and `delete_mount` detaches it. Whoever reads that agent reads the disk then, so a person attaches a disk to read only when they read it, and writable only when they write it, only where the agent's work needs what the disk holds. What someone attached goes once they no longer reach it.

**From your run** each named disk your person reads is a folder ``disks/<name>/`` of your sandbox, writable where they may write, and one shared with them on one folder alone shows that folder. An agent's run holds only the disks attached to its agent. Your instructions list each with its name, its description and whether you may write it, and when it holds an ``index.md``, the map of what it holds, read that before you work with the disk. While no curator keeps a disk, the runs that write it keep that map, one short line per file. What a run derives from a file to read it again faster, a transcript or a spreadsheet as CSV, goes in the disk's ``.cache/`` under the file's own path, which the app shows muted and anyone may clear. **Over the MCP** a named disk is a disk: `list_disks` with ``named`` true, then `list_files` on it, which answers each file's ``id``. `get_file` and `update_file` take that id as ``file_id``, and `create_file` takes the disk's ``disk_id`` and a ``path``.

## Finding what they hold

`search_workspace` with the question in ``q`` answers the files whose text answers it, ranked by its words, its meaning and the part or fault codes it names, best first, each with its passages that matched and the pages they span. Documents, scans, images, audio and video are searched too: their text, what a picture shows, what a recording says and what a video shows, are read for you, and a recording's passage names the seconds it spans (``second``, ``second_end``). Open the file there rather than reading every disk: `file_read` with ``pages`` on a PDF of your sandbox, or with ``seconds`` on a recording, or `get_file` over the MCP. From a run it searches the files of the disks your run mounts, and ``decide`` true keeps only the passages the Decisions step judges answer the question, billed to your run. Over the MCP it searches the files of the disks your person's Coordinator would mount, without ``decide``. Your person's own key and their Coordinator also search the descriptions and instructions of every agent and preset the person reads, so an agent is found by what it is for: ``type`` ``AGENT,PRESET`` keeps to them, each match naming its ``agent_id`` or ``preset_id`` and its ``name``. The search matches the files' own words, not the person's, so put the question in clear words in ``q`` and up to five short phrasings in ``variants``, the way the files would say it: in their language, with the part code as printed and the unit's name as the manual names it, and a symptom as the remedy a manual files it under (cleaning, adjustment, troubleshooting). All of them are ranked as one list, and ``decide`` judges the passages against ``q``. With ``decide``, no passage left means those words missed, not that the disks are silent: search again in other words before saying they hold nothing. An agent's instructions name `search_workspace` with no toolkit: each run is told which one holds it, `vibetasking` on a run someone starts and `platform` on a conversation nobody starts (a channel, a trigger nobody owns).

Answer from the files, and cite every one you used as a mention whose id is its ``file_id``: ``@[Shipping days](ref:file:<file_id>)``, with ``:<page>`` after the id for a page of a PDF: ``@[Manual, p. 41](ref:file:<file_id>:41)``, or ``@<second>`` for a moment of a recording: ``@[Kickoff call, 12:34](ref:file:<file_id>@754)``. The mention opens the file in the app, at that page or that moment. Say so when the files do not cover the question rather than filling the gap.

## Teaching it

When the person tells you something worth keeping ("we ship on Tuesdays"), write it where it belongs, with their authority:

- Find the page through the disk's ``index.md`` when it has one, and add a page under ``pages/`` only when none fits, linking it from ``index.md``.
- Choose the disk by what it is for and who reads it. What the person tells you about themselves and how they work with you goes to your own memory where you keep one (an agent keeps a memory of its own, which its runs write), and the documents and notes they keep for themselves to a disk only they read. Never copy one person's notes into a disk others read without asking them. When no disk fits, offer to make one rather than forcing it into another.
- Every claim cites where it came from, keeps its hedge, says who stated it and words its terms, prices, dates and figures as its source does: "Ana said on 3 October that we ship on Tuesdays", the conversation cited as ``@[title](ref:run:<id>)``, a document or another page as a link relative to the page (``../sources/contract.pdf``), a web page by its URL.
- A page opens with what holds now about its subject. When a fact changes, change it there and keep the old claim below, dated, rather than adding what was said to the end, since a page grown as a log stops saying what holds.
- While the brain's curator works, a run never writes its ``sources/``, ``index.md`` or ``people.md``: a document the person wants the brain to learn from, they add to it on the Knowledge page, and adding or replacing it wakes the curator.

## The brain

The brain is the named disk that holds what the company knows about itself, the one place its people and agents look things up. It is a beta an owner turns on for the workspace: `get_workspace` answers ``brain``, and `update_workspace` with ``brain`` true turns it on, a change a person makes, an owner's (**A change a person makes** in `configuration.md`). While it is off nobody makes a brain, and the brain's curator stops and its sources pause until it is on again. An owner then makes the brain with `create_disk` and ``brain`` true, one per workspace, and `list_disks` with ``named`` true shows which one it is (``variant`` ``BRAIN``).

Its **curator** keeps it consistent and up to date: it reads the brain's documents and its sources, writes its pages, ``index.md`` and ``people.md``, checks what people and conversations write there, keeps who reads and who writes its paths, and raises insights about what the brain holds. Only the brain has one. `update_disk` with ``curator`` true turns it on and false stops it, keeping its settings and sources. Its settings ride on the disk: ``instructions``, what its people ask of it in their own words, ``insights``, the kinds it raises, and ``open_insights``, how many each person holds open from it. Who manages the brain sets them, its sources and tasks too: the workspace's owners when the brain is the whole workspace's, never its admins unless an owner shares it with them to manage, else its member and whoever oversees them. They also decide who reads it: every seat while its share with the workspace stands, admins included, else only they and the people they share it with. A person who does not read the brain is told so in the app, and a workspace holds one brain, so no other can be made for them. The curator is no agent and acts for nobody: it reads the whole workspace as its owners do, changes nothing outside its brain, and never changes an automation. Only the workspace's owners read its runs. Speak of it as "the Brain", the way the app names it, never as an agent of theirs.

While it works the brain has its layout: ``sources/`` holds the documents people added, as they added them, which no run changes, the curator's included, ``pages/`` its pages, in folders by subject, ``index.md`` the short map of the brain, and ``people.md`` who's who, one entry per person the company deals with, each member's tied to their profile id. Only the curator's runs change ``index.md`` and ``people.md``, and on a brain of the whole workspace, among people only its owners, whoever else it is shared with to edit. Viewers and the conversations nobody starts never read ``people.md``. What changed on the brain, by whom and why, is its history: `list_edits` with the disk's ``disk_id``, and ``created_gte`` for what changed since a moment.

**Who reads what.** The curator keeps each subject with the people it belongs to through path rules of its own, by ``people.md``, what its instructions ask and the workspace's seats. It changes only the rules it set: one a person sets or changes is theirs. A page never cites or mentions a record some of its readers cannot open, so what the curator learned somewhere only some may read stays on a page kept to them.

It wakes when someone adds or replaces documents in the brain's ``sources/``, when a person answers one of its insights, when one of its sources or tasks fires, and on `start_curator` with the disk's ``disk_id``: with nothing else to read the brain again, or with a ``note`` saying what you found working from it (something it holds that is wrong or out of date, or something it was silent on), which the curator checks against the brain and your conversation before it changes a page. Hand it that rather than raising an insight about the brain yourself.

Its **sources and tasks** are triggers on the disk, set up with `create_trigger` and the disk's ``disk_id`` and listed with `list_disk_triggers`:

- **A scheduled task**: a ``cron`` (and a ``timezone``) for the cadence and a ``prompt`` saying what to do: research a topic and which pages it informs ("Research EU AI Act guidance that affects SMEs and update the relevant pages"), or review the brain ("Review the brain: pages that disagree, one subject kept on two pages, claims past their date, subjects with no page, threads worth following"). A source runs at most hourly, so its ``cron`` names one minute of the hour, and a task always takes a ``prompt``, since the curator's own instructions name no task. Each fire tells the curator when the source's last run to complete started.
- **A tool source**: the ``cron``, the ``integration_id`` of an integration the person reads, which is their consent to its reads, and a ``prompt`` saying what to read each time ("New documents in the Sales folder since the last check"). Each fire names that integration and its account to the curator, so the prompt says what to look for there, never which account, and without one the curator reads the account for whatever the brain keeps.
- **An asset source**: the same for a mailbox, a LinkedIn seat, a Stripe account, a phone number or a database, with the ``asset_id``, or the asset's event (``config.event``) or a poll of one of its tools, which wake the curator on what is new and need no hourly pace.
- **A channel source**: a channel the person reads, with its ``channel_id``. A ``cron`` reads it on a schedule, at most hourly and with no prompt needed: the curator reads the messages and the calls of every conversation on it since its last run, through `list_messages` and `list_calls`. Without a ``cron``, ``config.event`` wakes it on each message a contact sends (``message_received``) or each call that ends (``call_ended``), and a poll of one of the channel's read tools on what is new there. A busy channel is better read on a schedule than on every message.
- A source on events, an asset's, a channel's, a tool's or a poll's, may carry a ``config.condition``, in plain words what an event must be about for the curator to read it ("Only mail about a contract or an invoice"), so a busy mailbox or table wakes it only for what the brain keeps.
- The curator's runs hold the brain alone as a folder and read any other Knowledge disk through `list_files` and `get_file`, so a task that reads one names it by its mention, never by a ``disks/<name>/`` path.
- `update_trigger` changes a source's cadence or prompt, ``enabled`` false pauses it, and `delete_trigger` removes it.

Its **insights** are about the brain: ``CONTRADICTION`` (two of its documents disagree), ``RISK``, ``OPPORTUNITY``, ``RECOMMENDATION``, ``AUTOMATION`` (work an agent could take over, weighed against the agents that exist, found in what the brain describes and in jobs a person asked you for by hand more than once, whose conversations its details mention, along with the agent to extend when one does part of the work) and ``PROMPT`` (an agent the brain is attached to whose instructions contradict it). They reach the people the curator names as the ones a finding falls to, by ``people.md`` and its pages, else the brain's keepers, its owners for the whole workspace's, and everyone who reads the brain and what an insight cites reads it. When the person wants fewer, set ``insights`` on the brain with `update_disk` to the kinds to keep, an empty list for none, so it only keeps the pages, or ``open_insights`` to how many each person holds open from it at most, 10 when unset. The curator keeps its open insights current itself: it folds what adds to one into it and withdraws one that no longer holds, which then reads as dismissed with ``withdrawn:`` and its reason.

When the person answers one of them, close it with `review_insight` and a ``note`` holding what they said, whether it was done or does not apply: the curator reads it and brings the pages in line with it, citing their answer.
